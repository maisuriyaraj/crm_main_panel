# OrbitOps — Product Overview

## What is it?

OrbitOps is a business growth platform, built by RJ Industries, that brings together the tools a growing company normally has to buy separately — a CRM, a sales pipeline tool, marketing automation, ad campaign management, billing/invoicing, and business analytics — into one connected system.

The pitch, in the product's own words: *"Run your entire business from one platform."* Instead of a sales team using one tool, marketing using another, finance using a third, and nobody having a full picture of the customer, OrbitOps keeps everything — every lead, conversation, deal, invoice, and ad click — tied to a single customer record that every team can see.

## How is it different from similar products?

Most CRMs focus narrowly on contacts and deals, and leave you to bolt on separate tools for marketing automation, ad tracking, and billing — usually with clunky integrations and data that falls out of sync between systems. OrbitOps' main differentiator is being genuinely unified rather than integrated:

- **One customer record, not five.** A contact's emails, deals, invoices, and ad interactions all live on the same timeline, so any team member sees the complete history without switching tools.
- **Marketing and advertising are native, not bolted on.** Ad campaign management (e.g. Meta Ads) and attribution reporting live inside the same platform as the sales pipeline, so you can trace revenue back to the campaign that generated it — not just clicks.
- **Automation is no-code.** Follow-ups, lead nurturing, and trigger-based actions are built with a visual workflow builder, so non-technical teams can set up automations without engineering help.
- **Billing lives next to the CRM.** Subscription tracking, invoicing, and payment monitoring are part of the same system as the sales data that generated the revenue, instead of a disconnected accounting tool.
- **Reporting is live, not a periodic export.** Analytics and revenue dashboards reflect what's happening across sales, marketing, and billing in real time, rather than requiring manual report-building.

## Core features

OrbitOps is organized around six connected areas:

1. **CRM Management** — lead management, contact management, activity tracking, notes & attachments.
2. **Sales Pipeline** — Kanban-style pipelines, deal tracking, forecasting, follow-up management.
3. **Marketing Automation** — visual workflows, email sequences, lead nurturing, trigger-based actions.
4. **Advertising Platform** — ad campaign management (e.g. Meta Ads), campaign analytics, lead sync from ad platforms, ROI tracking.
5. **Billing & Invoicing** — subscription tracking, invoice generation, payment monitoring, revenue reporting.
6. **Business Analytics** — an executive dashboard, revenue insights, team performance, and conversion analytics.

Supporting these, the product also offers:

- A **customer 360° view** — a single timeline showing every activity, deal, and interaction for a given customer.
- **Cross-channel attribution** — connecting marketing/advertising spend to the revenue it actually produced.
- Guided **data migration** from existing CRMs (CSV import, with assisted migration on higher-tier plans).
- Integrations with common email, calendar, payment, advertising, and productivity tools, plus an API.
- A **book-a-demo** flow for prospects to schedule a personalized walkthrough, and a **free trial** (14 days, no credit card required) to get started directly.

## Who is it for?

OrbitOps targets small-to-midsize businesses and growing teams that have outgrown spreadsheets or a bare-bones CRM but don't want to stitch together (and pay for) five separate SaaS tools. It's built to serve multiple teams within one company at once — sales, marketing, and finance — rather than a single department.

The platform is presented as flexible enough to fit a range of industries, including:

- Real estate
- Healthcare
- Education
- Marketing/advertising agencies
- Finance
- SaaS companies
- Retail businesses
- Professional services

Customer testimonials in the product point to sales teams (fewer manual updates, more time closing), agencies (client reporting tied to real revenue, not just clicks), and operations leaders (more consistent, less-manual customer follow-up) as typical beneficiaries.
